### 2.1 The Neurophysiology of the Action Potential (Hodgkin & Huxley, 1952 Nobel Prize)
A neuron transmits signals electrically along its axon via voltage-gated ion channels:
1. **Resting Membrane Potential**: Maintained at **$-70\text{ mV}$** by the $\text{Na}^+/\text{K}^+$-ATPase pump (concentrating $\text{K}^+$ inside, $\text{Na}^+$ outside).
2. **Threshold**: Excitatory post-synaptic potentials depolarize the axon hillock to **$-55\text{ mV}$**.
3. **Depolarization**: Voltage-gated $\text{Na}^+$ channels snap open; a rush of sodium ions drives the membrane potential to **$+30\text{ mV}$** in $<1\text{ ms}$.
4. **Repolarization**: $\text{Na}^+$ channels inactivate; voltage-gated $\text{K}^+$ channels open, releasing potassium ions and restoring negative potential.
5. **Myelination & Saltatory Conduction**: Oligodendrocytes wrap axons in lipid myelin sheaths interrupted by uninsulated **Nodes of Ranvier**. The electrical impulse skips from node to node at up to **$120\text{ meters/second}$** ($432\text{ km/h}$).

## 3. The Cardiovascular & Respiratory Engines

### 3.1 The Human Heart: Hemodynamics and Cardiac Cycle
The heart beats approximately **100,000 times per day** (2.5 billion beats in an average lifetime), pumping **7,200 liters of blood daily**:
- **Right Heart**: Receives deoxygenated blood from the body via the Vena Cava and pumps it to the lungs via the Pulmonary Artery (low-pressure pulmonary circuit, $25/10\text{ mmHg}$).
- **Left Heart**: Receives oxygenated blood from the lungs via Pulmonary Veins and pumps it to the body via the Aorta (high-pressure systemic circuit, $120/80\text{ mmHg}$).
- **Intrinsic Pacemaker (SA Node)**: The Sinoatrial node in the right atrium spontaneously fires action potentials via hyperpolarization-activated cyclic nucleotide-gated (HCN) "funny" channels ($I_f$), setting a basal heart rate of 60–100 beats per minute.

### 3.2 The Respiratory System & Gas Exchange
- **Alveolar Architecture**: The lungs terminate in **300 to 500 million microscopic alveoli**, creating a total capillary gas exchange surface area of **$100\text{ square meters}$** (the size of a tennis court) with an ultra-thin barrier of only **$0.2\ \mu\text{m}$**.
- **Pulmonary Surfactant**: Type II alveolar cells secrete dipalmitoylphosphatidylcholine (DPPC), reducing alveolar surface tension from 70 to $<1\text{ mN/m}$, preventing smaller alveoli from collapsing into larger ones according to the **Law of Laplace**:
  $$P = \frac{2\gamma}{r}$$
- **Hemoglobin Allostery**: Each erythrocyte contains 270 million hemoglobin molecules. Hemoglobin binds up to 4 oxygen molecules cooperatively (sigmoidal binding curve). The **Bohr Effect** ensures that acidic, $\text{CO}_2$-rich metabolically active tissues trigger hemoglobin to unload oxygen precisely where it is needed most.

## 4. The Renal Engine: Master of Chemical Homeostasis

The two kidneys receive **20% to 25% of total cardiac output** (1.2 liters of blood per minute), filtering the entire blood volume 36 times every day:
- **The Nephron (1 Million per Kidney)**:
  1. **Glomerulus & Bowman's Capsule**: Glomerular capillary blood pressure forces water and small solutes through a three-layer fenestrated filter into Bowman's space, generating a **Glomerular Filtration Rate (GFR) of $180\text{ liters/day}$**.
  2. **Proximal Convoluted Tubule**: Obligatorily reabsorbs $65\%$ of all filtered water, sodium, potassium, and $100\%$ of filtered glucose and amino acids.
  3. **Loop of Henle (Countercurrent Multiplier)**: The descending limb is permeable to water; the ascending limb actively pumps $\text{Na}^+$ and $\text{Cl}^-$ out into the medulla, establishing a steep osmotic gradient ($300\text{ mOsm/kg}$ at cortex to $1,200\text{ mOsm/kg}$ in deep medulla).
  4. **Collecting Duct**: Under the control of **Antidiuretic Hormone (ADH / Vasopressin)**, aquaporin channels insert into the membrane, allowing the body to reabsorb water and concentrate urine up to $1,200\text{ mOsm/kg}$, preventing fatal dehydration.
- Out of 180 liters filtered daily, **$178.5\text{ liters}$ are reabsorbed**, and only $1.5\text{ liters}$ are excreted as concentrated urine containing urea, creatinine, and excess electrolytes.

## 5. The Musculoskeletal System & The Sliding Filament Engine

The human skeleton contains **206 bones** providing structural leverage, protecting vital organs, and acting as a calcium reservoir.
- **Bone Remodeling**: Dynamic balance between bone-destroying **Osteoclasts** (secreting hydrochloric acid and cathepsin K to dissolve bone mineral) and bone-building **Osteoblasts** (secreting Type I collagen and alkaline phosphatase to mineralize hydroxyapatite crystals $\text{Ca}_{10}(\text{PO}_4)_6(\text{OH})_2$). Every human replaces their entire skeleton roughly once every 10 years!
- **The Sliding Filament Theory of Muscle Contraction (Hugh Huxley & Andrew Huxley, 1954)**:
  1. Motor neuron action potential releases **Acetylcholine** at the neuromuscular junction.
  2. Depolarization travels down T-tubules, opening ryanodine receptors to release $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ from the sarcoplasmic reticulum.
  3. $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ binds to **Troponin C**, causing **Tropomyosin** to shift away from actin active binding sites.
  4. **Cross-Bridge Cycle**: Myosin heads hydrolyze ATP ($\text{ATP} \to \text{ADP} + P_i$), cock into a high-energy state, bind to actin, and execute the **Power Stroke** (pulling actin filaments inward by $10\text{ nm}$ per stroke), shortening the sarcomere and generating mechanical force.
  5. Binding of a new ATP molecule detaches the myosin head. In death, ATP depletion prevents detachment, locking muscles in **Rigor Mortis**.
