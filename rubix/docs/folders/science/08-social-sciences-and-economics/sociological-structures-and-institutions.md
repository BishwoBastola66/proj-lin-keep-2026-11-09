### 1.1 Émile Durkheim: Social Facts and Functional Cohesion
Durkheim established sociology as an autonomous empirical science:
1. **Social Facts (*Faits Sociaux*)**: External forces capable of exercising coercive control over individuals (laws, customs, religious beliefs, currency). *"Treat social facts as things."*
2. **Evolution of Social Solidarity (*The Division of Labour in Society*, 1893)**:
   - **Mechanical Solidarity**: Pre-industrial societies characterized by low division of labor, strong collective conscience, and repressive punitive law.
   - **Organic Solidarity**: Industrialized societies characterized by high division of labor, mutual interdependency, functional specialization, and restitutive law.
3. **Anomie and Empirical Suicide Study (1897)**:
   When rapid economic shifts disrupt normative frameworks, individuals experience **anomie** (normlessness, breakdown of societal regulation).
   - Classified suicide into four structural types: *Anomic* (lack of regulation), *Egoistic* (low social integration), *Altruistic* (extreme social integration, e.g., kamikaze), and *Fatalistic* (excessive regulation).

### 1.2 Max Weber: Rationalization and Authority
1. **Methodological Individualism & *Verstehen***: Sociological investigation must interpret the subjective meanings individuals attach to their actions.
2. **The Protestant Ethic and the Spirit of Capitalism (1905)**:
   Demonstrated that religious cultural ideas can drive economic systems. Calvinist doctrines of predestination generated ascetic worldly work ethics and capital accumulation.
3. **Rationalization and the "Iron Cage" (*Stahlhartes Gehäuse*)**:
   Modernity replaces traditional, mystical values with calculability, formal rationality, and efficiency, trapping human agency inside bureaucratic systems.
4. **The Three Pure Types of Legitimate Authority**:
   - **Traditional Authority**: Sanctity of immemorial traditions and patriarchal hereditary rule.
   - **Charismatic Authority**: Exceptional individual heroism, sanctity, or exemplary character (inherently unstable, requires *routinization*).
   - **Legal-Rational Authority**: Legality of enacted formal rules and systemic right of those elevated to authority to issue commands (bureaucracy).

### 1.3 Karl Marx: Historical Materialism & Capital Dynamics
1. **Base and Superstructure**:
   The economic **Base** (relations of production + forces of production) conditions the legal, political, philosophical, and religious **Superstructure**.
2. **Dialectical Materialism**: Human history is the history of class struggles driven by material contradictions (Free man/Slave, Patrician/Plebeian, Lord/Serf, Bourgeoisie/Proletariat).
3. **The Four Dimensions of Alienation (*Entfremdung*)**:
   Under capitalist wage labor, the worker is estranged:
   - From the product of labor (it belongs to the capitalist).
   - From the act of production (work is monotonous and coerced).
   - From *Gattungswesen* (human species-essence; conscious, creative life-activity).
   - From other workers (converted into market competitors).

### 2.1 Robert K. Merton: Middle-Range Theories
1. **Manifest vs. Latent Functions**:
   - *Manifest Functions*: Conscious, intended, publicly recognized consequences of a social practice.
   - *Latent Functions*: Unintended, unrecognized consequences (e.g., the Hopi rain dance fostering social solidarity, or higher education functioning as marriage matchmaking).
2. **Strain Theory of Deviance**:
   Deviance emerges when a cultural gap opens between culturally defined goals (e.g., wealth) and institutionalized legitimate means to attain them:
   - *Conformity* (+Goals, +Means), *Innovation* (+Goals, -Means, e.g., crime), *Ritualism* (-Goals, +Means, robotic bureaucracy), *Retreatism* (-Goals, -Means), *Rebellion* (substituting new goals and means).
