### 1.1 The Quantum Mechanical Atom: The Four Quantum Numbers
An electron does not orbit the nucleus like a planet. It occupies a 3D stationary probability wave (orbital) defined by four quantum numbers:
1. **Principal Quantum Number ($n = 1, 2, 3, \dots$)**: Defines the energy level and radial distance of the shell from the nucleus.
2. **Azimuthal / Angular Momentum Quantum Number ($l = 0, 1, \dots, n-1$)**: Defines orbital shape:
   - $l = 0$: **$s$ orbital** (Spherical, holds 2 electrons).
   - $l = 1$: **$p$ orbital** (Dumbbell-shaped, 3 orientations, holds 6 electrons).
   - $l = 2$: **$d$ orbital** (Cloverleaf-shaped, 5 orientations, holds 10 electrons).
   - $l = 3$: **$f$ orbital** (Complex nodal surfaces, 7 orientations, holds 14 electrons).
3. **Magnetic Quantum Number ($m_l = -l, \dots, 0, \dots, +l$)**: Defines spatial orientation of the orbital in space.
4. **Spin Quantum Number ($m_s = +\frac{1}{2}, -\frac{1}{2}$)**: Defines intrinsic quantum angular momentum of the electron.

### 1.2 Electron Configuration Rules
- **Aufbau Principle**: Electrons fill the lowest-energy orbitals first ($1s \to 2s \to 2p \to 3s \to 3p \to 4s \to 3d \dots$).
- **Pauli Exclusion Principle (1925)**: No two electrons in an atom can have the exact same set of four quantum numbers. Each spatial orbital holds a maximum of 2 electrons with opposite spins ($\uparrow\downarrow$).
- **Hund’s Rule of Maximum Multiplicity**: When filling degenerate orbitals of equal energy (like $2p_x, 2p_y, 2p_z$), electrons occupy separate orbitals with parallel spins before pairing up, minimizing electron-electron electrostatic repulsion.

## 2. Dmitri Mendeleev’s Periodic Law & Modern Periodic Trends

In 1869, Russian chemist Dmitri Mendeleev arranged the known 63 elements by increasing atomic weight and chemical valence. He discovered that chemical properties recur **periodically**:
> *"The properties of the elements are a periodic function of their atomic weights."* (Later refined by Henry Moseley in 1913 to **Atomic Number $Z$** = number of protons).

Mendeleev famously predicted the existence and exact properties of undiscovered elements (Gallium, Scandium, Germanium) decades before they were isolated.

### 2.1 The Periodic Families
- **Alkali Metals (Group 1: Li, Na, K, Rb, Cs, Fr)**: 1 valence electron ($ns^1$). Violently reactive with water, forming strong hydroxides and releasing hydrogen gas.
- **Alkaline Earth Metals (Group 2: Be, Mg, Ca, Sr, Ba, Ra)**: 2 valence electrons ($ns^2$). Basic oxides, reactive metals.
- **Transition Metals (d-block: Fe, Cu, Au, Ag, Pt, Ti)**: Incompletely filled $d$ subshells. Multiple oxidation states, colorful coordination complexes, paramagnetic properties, industrial catalysts.
- **Halogens (Group 17: F, Cl, Br, I, At)**: 7 valence electrons ($ns^2 np^5$). Highly electronegative; aggressively accept 1 electron to form stable halide anions.
- **Noble Gases (Group 18: He, Ne, Ar, Kr, Xe, Rn)**: Completely filled valence octet ($ns^2 np^6$). Extreme chemical inertness; zero electron affinity.
- **Lanthanides & Actinides (f-block)**: Rare-earth magnetic elements, and radioactive heavy elements (Uranium, Plutonium).
