### 1.1 Cosmic Inflation (Alan Guth, Andrei Linde, 1980–1981)
Prior to inflation, the standard Big Bang model faced two fatal paradoxes:
1. **The Horizon Problem**: Why is the Cosmic Microwave Background (CMB) temperature identical to 1 part in 100,000 across opposite sides of the sky that were never in causal contact?
2. **The Flatness Problem**: Why is the spatial geometry of the universe measured to be extraordinarily flat ($\Omega_k \approx 0$)?
- **The Inflationary Solution**: A scalar field (the **Inflaton**) with negative pressure drove an exponential metric expansion of spacetime between $10^{-36}$ and $10^{-32}$ seconds. Spacetime expanded by at least $e^{60} \approx 10^{26}$, stretching quantum fluctuations into macroscopic density perturbations that seeded all stars, galaxies, and cosmic web clusters.

### 1.2 The Cosmic Microwave Background (CMB)
Discovered in 1965 by Arno Penzias and Robert Wilson (1978 Nobel Prize):
- Prior to 379,000 years after the Big Bang, temperatures were above 3,000 K, creating an opaque plasma of free electrons and protons. Photons were perpetually trapped by Thomson scattering.
- At $T \approx 3,000\text{ K}$, electrons combined with protons to form neutral hydrogen (**Recombination**). Photons decoupled and travelled freely through space for the first time.
- Over 13.8 billion years, the cosmic expansion has stretched these photons' wavelengths by a factor of 1,100, cooling them to a blackbody radiation temperature of **$2.7255\text{ Kelvin}$** in the microwave spectrum.
- The CMB represents the oldest observable light in the universe—a direct snapshot of the baby cosmos.

### 2.1 Dark Matter: The Invisible Skeleton of Galaxies
- First inferred by Fritz Zwicky (1933) in the Coma galaxy cluster and confirmed by **Vera Rubin (1970)** observing galactic rotation curves: stars at the outer edges of spiral galaxies orbit just as fast as stars near the center, violating Keplerian orbital dynamics unless a massive, invisible halo of dark matter envelopes the galaxy.
- Dark matter does not emit, absorb, or reflect electromagnetic radiation (light), but its gravitational pull bends light via **Gravitational Lensing**.

### 2.2 Dark Energy & The Accelerating Universe
- In 1998, Saul Perlmutter, Brian Schmidt, and Adam Riess measured Type Ia supernovae distances, discovering that **the expansion of the universe is not slowing down under gravity; it is accelerating**.
- Driven by **Dark Energy**, modeled as Einstein’s Cosmological Constant $\Lambda$ in General Relativity, with an equation of state $w = \frac{p}{\rho} \approx -1$.
- Because dark energy density remains constant as space expands, as galaxies move further apart, dark energy will increasingly dominate, driving the universe asymptotically toward the **Big Freeze** (Heat Death).
