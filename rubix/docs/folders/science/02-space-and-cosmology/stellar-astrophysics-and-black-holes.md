### 1.1 Hydrostatic Equilibrium & Nuclear Fusion
A star exists in a delicate balance between two opposing forces:
$$\frac{dP}{dr} = -\frac{G M(r) \rho(r)}{r^2}$$
1. **Inward Force**: The relentless gravitational attraction of all the star’s mass pulling toward the core.
2. **Outward Force**: Thermal gas pressure and radiation pressure generated by nuclear fusion reactions in the core.

- **Proton-Proton (p-p) Chain (Stars $\le 1.3 M_{\odot}$, like the Sun)**:
  $$4 \, ^1\text{H} \longrightarrow \, ^4\text{He} + 2e^+ + 2\nu_e + 26.73\text{ MeV}$$
  Converts 0.7% of the mass of hydrogen into energy according to $E = mc^2$. Every second, the Sun converts 600 million tons of hydrogen into helium.
- **CNO Cycle (Stars $> 1.3 M_{\odot}$)**: Carbon, Nitrogen, and Oxygen act as nuclear catalysts at core temperatures $>15\text{ million K}$, scaling reaction rates with temperature as $T^{16}$ to $T^{20}$.

### 1.2 The Death of Sun-Like Stars: White Dwarfs & The Chandrasekhar Limit
When a star with mass $<8 M_{\odot}$ exhausts hydrogen in its core:
1. Core contracts and heats up; outer envelope expands into a **Red Giant**.
2. Helium fuses into Carbon and Oxygen (the Triple-Alpha process).
3. The outer layers are shed as a glowing **Planetary Nebula**, leaving behind the naked, ultra-dense core: a **White Dwarf**.
- **Electron Degeneracy Pressure**: A white dwarf has no nuclear fusion. It is prevented from collapsing under gravity solely by the **Pauli Exclusion Principle**: electrons cannot occupy the same quantum state.
- **The Chandrasekhar Limit (Subrahmanyan Chandrasekhar, 1930 Nobel Prize)**:
  $$M_{\text{Ch}} \approx 1.44 \, M_{\odot}$$
  If a white dwarf accretes mass from a companion star and exceeds $1.44 M_{\odot}$, the speed of its degenerate electrons approaches the speed of light ($c$), relativistic electron degeneracy pressure collapses, and the white dwarf detonates in a thermonuclear explosion: a **Type Ia Supernova**.

## 2. Massive Stars, Supernovae & Heavy Element Nucleosynthesis

For massive stars ($>8 M_{\odot}$), the core temperature rises high enough to fuse progressively heavier elements in concentric onion-like shells:
$$\text{Hydrogen} \longrightarrow \text{Helium} \longrightarrow \text{Carbon} \longrightarrow \text{Neon} \longrightarrow \text{Oxygen} \longrightarrow \text{Silicon} \longrightarrow \text{Iron-56}$$

### 2.1 The Iron Catastrophe & Core Collapse
- **Iron-56 has the highest nuclear binding energy per nucleon** of any element (8.8 MeV per nucleon).
- Fusing elements lighter than iron releases energy (exothermic). Fusing elements heavier than iron consumes energy (endothermic).
- When the core becomes pure Iron, fusion stops producing outward radiation pressure. In **less than 100 milliseconds**, gravity collapses the entire core of the star from a sphere the size of Earth down to a city-sized ball of 20 kilometers radius.
- Protons and electrons are crushed together by gravity to form neutrons (**Electron Capture**):
  $$p + e^- \longrightarrow n + \nu_e$$
- A cataclysmic blast wave rebounds off the incompressible neutron core, ejecting the outer layers into space at $10\%$ the speed of light: a **Type II Supernova**.
- **Stellar Nucleosynthesis**: All gold, platinum, and uranium on Earth were synthesized in the violent neutron flux of supernovae (**r-process**) and colliding neutron star mergers (kilonovae). As Carl Sagan famously stated: *"We are made of starstuff."*

### 3.1 Neutron Stars: Nuclear Density in Astronomical Objects
- A neutron star packs more mass than the Sun into a sphere 12 kilometers across.
- **Density**: A single teaspoon of neutron star material has a mass of **6 billion tons** (equivalent to a mountain).
- **Tolman-Oppenheimer-Volkoff (TOV) Limit**: The maximum theoretical mass of a non-rotating neutron star supported by neutron degeneracy pressure is **$\sim 2.1$ to $2.3 M_{\odot}$**. Above this limit, no force in nature can stop total gravitational collapse into a black hole.

### 3.2 Black Holes: Spacetime Singularities & Horizons
When a stellar core exceeds the TOV limit, gravity collapses it past its **Schwarzschild Radius ($r_s$)**:
$$r_s = \frac{2 G M}{c^2}$$
For Earth, $r_s \approx 9\text{ millimeters}$; for the Sun, $r_s \approx 3\text{ kilometers}$.

- **The Event Horizon**: The spherical boundary of a black hole where the escape velocity equals the speed of light ($c$). Nothing—neither light nor matter nor information—can cross from the inside to the outside.
- **The Singularity**: According to General Relativity (Penrose-Hawking Singularity Theorems, 2020 Nobel Prize to Roger Penrose), all mass inside the event horizon collapses to an infinitesimal point of infinite density and infinite curvature, where classical General Relativity breaks down.
- **Kerr Black Holes**: Rotating black holes feature an **Ergosphere** outside the event horizon where spacetime is dragged around the black hole at $>c$ (Frame Dragging / Lense-Thirring effect). Energy can be extracted from the rotation via the **Penrose Process**.
- **Hawking Radiation (Stephen Hawking, 1974)**:
  Quantum fluctuations near the event horizon cause virtual particle-antiparticle pairs to separate; one falls in while the other escapes, causing black holes to slowly radiate energy and evaporate:
  $$T_H = \frac{\hbar c^3}{8\pi G M k_B}$$
  A stellar-mass black hole has a temperature of $60\text{ nanokelvin}$ and takes $10^{67}$ years to evaporate.
- **Gravitational Wave Astronomy (LIGO, 2015)**: Confirmed the merger of two black holes (GW150914) 1.3 billion light-years away, radiating 3 solar masses of pure gravitational wave energy in 0.2 seconds.
