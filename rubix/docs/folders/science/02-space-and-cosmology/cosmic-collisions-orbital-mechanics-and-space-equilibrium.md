### 1.1 Hypervelocity Impact Thermodynamics & Shock Waves
When celestial bodies collide, their relative velocity always exceeds the speed of sound in planetary rocks ($c_s \approx 5\text{--}8\text{ km/s}$):
$$v_{\text{imp}} = \sqrt{v_\infty^2 + v_{\text{esc}}^2} = \sqrt{v_\infty^2 + \frac{2G(M_1 + M_2)}{R_1 + R_2}}$$
At impact velocities of $15\text{--}40\text{ km/s}$, kinetic energy is partitioned into shock waves governed by the **Rankine-Hugoniot Jump Conditions**:
1. **Conservation of Mass**: $\rho_0 u_s = \rho (u_s - u_p)$
2. **Conservation of Momentum**: $P - P_0 = \rho_0 u_s u_p$
3. **Conservation of Energy**: $E - E_0 = \frac{1}{2}(P + P_0)\left(\frac{1}{\rho_0} - \frac{1}{\rho}\right)$
Where $u_s$ is shock wave speed, $u_p$ is particle speed, $\rho_0$ is uncompressed rock density, and $P$ is peak shock pressure. Pressures reach **$100\text{ to }1{,}000\text{ GPa}$**, instantly vaporizing thousands of cubic kilometers of silicate rock and iron.

### 1.2 The Synestia Discovery (Sarah Stewart & Simon Lock, 2017)
The classical view held that the Mars-sized protoplanet **Theia** struck the proto-Earth, ejecting a neat disk of debris that coalesced into the Moon. However, isotopic identicalness between Earth and Moon rocks contradicted this until the discovery of the **Synestia**:
- A giant impact transferring sufficient angular momentum spins the planet beyond its **corotation limit** (the equator tries to rotate faster than orbital velocity).
- The colliding bodies vaporize into a giant, rapidly spinning, doughnut-shaped mass of vaporized rock and liquid droplets: a **Synestia**.
- The Moon condensed from magma raindrops inside the outer rim of the Earth-Synestia over dozens of years, naturally explaining why Earth and Moon possess identical oxygen and titanium isotopic ratios.

### 1.3 The Roche Limit and Ring Formation
When an icy moon or comet strays too close to a massive planet, the differential tidal gravitational force across the moon exceeds its self-gravitational tensile cohesion:
$$d_R \approx 2.44 \cdot R_M \left(\frac{\rho_M}{\rho_m}\right)^{1/3}$$
Where $R_M$ is the primary planet's radius, $\rho_M$ is the planet's density, and $\rho_m$ is the satellite's density.
- Any loose satellite inside $d_R$ is **tidally ripped apart into billions of fragments**.
- This is the origin of **Saturn's rings** (an icy moon shattered within the Roche limit $\sim 100\text{ million years ago}$) and the fate of Comet Shoemaker-Levy 9, which Jupiter's tidal forces ripped into 21 fragments before impact in July 1994.

### 2.1 Hydrostatic Equilibrium
Every stable planet and star in the universe exists in hydrostatic balance. Gravity continuously pulls every parcel of matter inward toward the center, countered precisely by the outward **pressure gradient force**:
$$\frac{dP}{dr} = -\frac{G M(r) \rho(r)}{r^2}$$
- If thermal energy decreases, gravity compresses the core, raising temperature and core pressure until balance is restored.
- In white dwarf stars, outward pressure is supplied by **Electron Degeneracy Pressure** ($P \propto \rho^{5/3}$).
- In neutron stars, it is supplied by **Neutron Degeneracy Pressure** and strong nuclear repulsive forces.

### 2.2 The Virial Theorem (Rudolf Clausius, 1870)
For any stable, bound gravitational system of $N$ interacting particles in equilibrium:
$$2\langle K \rangle + \langle U \rangle = 0 \implies \langle K \rangle = -\frac{1}{2} \langle U \rangle$$
Where $\langle K \rangle$ is time-averaged kinetic energy and $\langle U \rangle$ is gravitational potential energy.
- **Fritz Zwicky's 1933 Discovery of Dark Matter**: Applying the Virial Theorem to the Coma Galaxy Cluster, Zwicky measured the velocity dispersion of galaxies ($\langle K \rangle$) and proved the visible luminous mass was 400 times too small to provide the required gravitational binding energy ($\langle U \rangle$). Cosmic clusters were held in balance by **Dark Matter**.

### 3.1 Joseph-Louis Lagrange's Three-Body Solution (1772)
In the circular restricted three-body problem, there exist exactly five positions where the gravitational forces of two massive bodies ($M_1$ and $M_2$) combine with the centrifugal force to create a stationary frame:
1. **Collinear Points ($L_1, L_2, L_3$)**:
   - Lie along the axis connecting the two masses.
   - **Dynamically Unstable**: Saddle points in effective gravitational potential; objects drift away without active station-keeping propulsion.
   - **$L_1$**: Home to solar observation satellites (SOHO, DSCOVR) between Sun and Earth.
   - **$L_2$**: $1.5\text{ million km}$ behind Earth away from the Sun; home to the James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) shielded from solar and terrestrial heat.
2. **Triangular Points ($L_4, L_5$)**:
   - Form equilateral triangles with $M_1$ and $M_2$, leading and trailing $M_2$ by $60^\circ$ along its orbit.
   - **Dynamically Stable**: Stabilized by the **Coriolis Force**, provided the mass ratio satisfies **Routh's Stability Criterion**:
     $$\mu = \frac{M_2}{M_1 + M_2} < \frac{1}{2}\left(1 - \sqrt{\frac{23}{27}}\right) \approx 0.03852$$
     (Sun-Jupiter $\mu \approx 0.00095$, well below the threshold).
   - Holds thousands of **Trojan Asteroids** locked stably in Jupiter's orbit for 4.5 billion years.

### 4.1 The Laplace Resonance & Tidal Heating
Pierre-Simon Laplace proved that the orbits of Io, Europa, and Ganymede are locked:
$$\phi_L = \lambda_{\text{Io}} - 3\lambda_{\text{Europa}} + 2\lambda_{\text{Ganymede}} = 180^\circ$$
Because $\phi_L$ never equals $0^\circ$, all three satellites can never line up on the same side of Jupiter simultaneously. This continuous resonance prevents orbital circularization, forcing Io's interior to undergo constant tidal flexing that generates **$100\text{ TW}$ of tidal geothermal heat**, making Io the most volcanically active body in the Solar System.

### 4.2 Cosmic-Scale Balance: Gravity vs. Dark Energy Expansion
On the grandest cosmic scale, the balance of the universe is governed by the Friedmann Equation:
$$H^2 = \left(\frac{\dot{a}}{a}\right)^2 = \frac{8\pi G}{3}\rho - \frac{k c^2}{a^2} + \frac{\Lambda c^2}{3}$$
- On small scales ($<10\text{ Megaparsecs}$), mutual gravity decouples star clusters and galaxies from the cosmic expansion, maintaining stable bound halos.
- On scales ($>100\text{ Megaparsecs}$), **Dark Energy ($\Lambda$)** dominates, driving accelerating cosmic expansion that prevents the universe from collapsing into a catastrophic "Big Crunch".
