### 1.1 The "Big Five" Mass Extinction Events
1. **End-Ordovician (444 Ma)**: Glaciation in Gondwana dropped sea levels and cooled oceans; 85% of marine species eliminated.
2. **Late Devonian (375 Ma)**: Ocean anoxia and algal blooms; wiped out 75% of species including reef-building corals.
3. **End-Permian "The Great Dying" (252 Ma)**: The most catastrophic extinction in Earth's history. Massive flood basalt eruptions in the **Siberian Traps** released gigatons of $\text{CO}_2$, acidifying oceans, heating the atmosphere by $8^\circ\text{C}$, and releasing toxic hydrogen sulfide gas. **$96\%$ of all marine species and $70\%$ of terrestrial vertebrate species were extinguished**.
4. **End-Triassic (201 Ma)**: Magmatism from the opening of the Atlantic Ocean; paved the way for dinosaur dominance.
5. **Cretaceous-Paleogene (K-Pg) Extinction (66 Ma)**: A 10-kilometer asteroid struck Chicxulub, Mexico, creating a 180 km crater. The impact triggered global firestorms, mega-tsunamis, and a nuclear winter that blocked sunlight for years, wiping out $75\%$ of all species, including all non-avian dinosaurs.

## 2. The Five Great Ice Ages of Earth

An **Ice Age** (Glacial Era) is a prolonged geological period in which permanent continental ice sheets cover Earth's polar and temperate regions.

```
+───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────+
|                                    THE FIVE MAJOR ICE AGES IN EARTH HISTORY                                   |
+──────────────────────────+────────────────────+───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────+
| Ice Age Name             | Geological Era     | Primary Trigger & Paleoclimatic Mechanism                     |
+──────────────────────────+────────────────────+───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────+
| 1. Huronian Glaciation   | 2.4 to 2.1 Gy ago  | Great Oxidation Event: Oxygen consumed atmospheric methane CH₄|
| 2. Cryogenian (Snowball) | 720 to 635 Ma      | Extreme runaway ice-albedo feedback; ice reached equator      |
| 3. Andean-Saharan        | 450 to 420 Ma      | South polar drift of Gondwana supercontinent                  |
| 4. Karoo Ice Age         | 360 to 260 Ma      | Carboniferous rainforests buried gigatons of carbon into coal |
| 5. Quaternary Glaciation | 2.58 Ma to Present | Isthmus of Panama closure + Milankovitch orbital cycles       |
+───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────+
```

### 2.1 The Cryogenian "Snowball Earth" (720 to 635 Million Years Ago)
The most severe glaciation in Earth’s history:
- **Runaway Ice-Albedo Feedback**: As glaciers advanced toward temperate latitudes, white ice reflected incoming solar radiation back into space (high albedo $\sim 0.8$). This cooled the planet further, driving glaciers all the way to the equator.
- Oceans froze to a depth of **1 kilometer** globally.
- **How It Ended**: The hydrological cycle stopped, shutting down chemical silicate weathering (which normally removes $\text{CO}_2$ from the air). Subaerial volcanoes continued to pump $\text{CO}_2$ into the atmosphere unabated for millions of years. Eventually, atmospheric $\text{CO}_2$ reached $13\%$ (350 times modern levels), generating a massive greenhouse effect that melted the ice sheets within a few thousand years.

### 2.2 The Quaternary Ice Age & Milankovitch Cycles
We currently live within the **Quaternary Ice Age** (which began 2.58 million years ago with the formation of the Arctic ice cap). We are living in an **Interglacial Period** (the Holocene, which began 11,700 years ago).
- **The Milankovitch Cycles (Milutin Milanković, 1920)**: Variations in Earth's orbit regulate the timing of glacial vs. interglacial cycles:
  1. **Orbital Eccentricity (100,000-year cycle)**: Earth’s orbit shifts from circular to elliptical ($e = 0.0005$ to $0.06$).
  2. **Axial Obliquity / Tilt (41,000-year cycle)**: Axial tilt oscillates between **22.1° and 24.5°** (currently at 23.44°). Lower tilt reduces summer heat at the poles, allowing winter snow to survive year-round.
  3. **Axial Precession (26,000-year cycle)**: The wobble of Earth's rotational axis alters which hemisphere experiences summer at perihelion (closest to the Sun).
- **Last Glacial Maximum (LGM, ~20,000 years ago)**:
  - Laurentide Ice Sheet (3 km thick) covered Canada and extended south to New York and Chicago.
  - Global sea levels were **120 meters (390 feet) lower** than today.
  - The Bering Land Bridge connected Asia to North America, enabling human migration into the Americas.
