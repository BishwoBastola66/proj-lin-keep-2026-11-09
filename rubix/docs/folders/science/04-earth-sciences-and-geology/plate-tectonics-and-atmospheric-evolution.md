## 1. Plate Tectonics: The Dynamic Surface of Earth

```
+───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────+
|                                    THE THREE TYPES OF PLATE BOUNDARIES                                        |
+──────────────────────────+────────────────────────────────────+───────────────────────────────────────────────+
| Boundary Type            | Geological Mechanism               | Topographic Features & Historic Examples      |
+──────────────────────────+────────────────────────────────────+───────────────────────────────────────────────+
| 1. Divergent (Construct.)| Plates pull apart; magma wells up  | Mid-Atlantic Ridge, East African Rift Valley  |
| 2. Convergent (Destruct.)| Plates collide; subduction or fold | Mariana Trench (deepest, 11km), Himalayas     |
| 3. Transform (Conservat.)| Plates slide horizontally past each| San Andreas Fault (California), Alpine Fault  |
+──────────────────────────+────────────────────────────────────+───────────────────────────────────────────────+
```

### 1.1 From Alfred Wegener to Modern Plate Tectonics
- **Alfred Wegener’s Continental Drift (1912)**:
  - Observed that the coastlines of South America and Africa fit together like puzzle pieces.
  - Discovered identical fossil species (*Mesosaurus*, *Glossopteris*) on continents separated by vast oceans, and matching glacial striations across modern tropical zones.
  - **Rejection**: Geologists rejected Wegener's theory because he could not propose a physical mechanism capable of plowing solid continents through the ocean floor.
- **The Seafloor Spreading Revolution (Harry Hess, 1960; Marie Tharp)**:
  - Marie Tharp mapped the ocean floor, discovering the continuous 65,000 km **Mid-Ocean Ridge** system with a central rift valley.
  - Harry Hess hypothesized that new oceanic crust forms at mid-ocean ridges and spreads outward.
  - **Paleomagnetic Striping (Vine-Matthews-Morley, 1963)**: Earth's magnetic field reverses polarity periodically. As magma cools at mid-ocean ridges, iron minerals lock in the current magnetic orientation. Symmetrical zebra-stripe magnetic bands on either side of ridges provided undeniable empirical proof that the seafloor spreads at **2 to 10 cm per year**.

### 1.2 What Drives the Plates?
1. **Slab Pull (Dominant Driving Force)**: As oceanic lithosphere cools away from a ridge, it becomes denser than the underlying asthenosphere. At subduction zones, the dense cold slab sinks into the mantle under gravity, pulling the entire trailing plate behind it.
2. **Ridge Push**: Magma buoyancy at elevated ridges exerts a gravitational sliding force outward.
3. **Mantle Convection Plumes**: Hot buoyant rock rises from the core-mantle boundary (D'' layer) in stationary plumes (**Hotspots** like Hawaii and Yellowstone), burning volcanic chains through moving tectonic plates.

## 2. The Evolution of Earth's Atmosphere

Earth's modern atmosphere ($78.08\%\ \text{N}_2, \, 20.95\%\ \text{O}_2, \, 0.93\%\ \text{Ar}, \, 0.042\%\ \text{CO}_2$) is not primordial; it was created and transformed by planetary volcanic outgassing and biological life:

```
+───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────+
|                                  THE THREE ATMOSPHERES OF PLANET EARTH                                        |
+──────────────────────────+────────────────────+───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────+
| Atmosphere Stage         | Geological Epoch   | Atmospheric Composition & Characteristics                     |
+──────────────────────────+────────────────────+───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────+
| 1. Primordial Atmosphere | 4.54 Gy ago        | Hydrogen, Helium (quickly lost to space via solar wind)       |
| 2. Secondary Atmosphere  | 4.4 to 2.45 Gy ago | H₂O vapor (condensed to oceans), CO₂ (huge), N₂, SO₂, CH₄, H₂S|
| 3. Modern Oxygenated Atm.| 2.45 Gy to Present | N₂ (78%), O₂ (21%), Argon (0.9%), Ozone layer (O₃) shield     |
+───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────+
```

### 2.1 The Great Oxidation Event (GOE, ~2.45 Billion Years Ago)
For the first two billion years of Earth's history, there was virtually **zero free oxygen ($\text{O}_2 < 0.001\%$)** in the atmosphere.
- **Oxygenic Photosynthesis**: Around 2.7 billion years ago, **Cyanobacteria** evolved the ability to split water molecules using solar photons:
  $$6\text{CO}_2 + 6\text{H}_2\text{O} + \text{photons} \longrightarrow \text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6 + 6\text{O}_2$$
- **Banded Iron Formations (BIFs)**: Initially, all released oxygen reacted immediately with dissolved ferrous iron ($\text{Fe}^{2+}$) in primordial oceans, precipitating out billions of tons of insoluble hematite and magnetite ($\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3, \text{Fe}_3\text{O}_4$) onto the seafloor (the source of all iron ore mined today).
- **The Catastrophe for Anaerobes**: Once oceanic iron was exhausted (~2.45 Ga), oxygen bubbled into the atmosphere. Because oxygen was violently toxic to obligate anaerobic organisms, the GOE caused the **first mass extinction of life** on Earth.
- **The Ozone Shield ($\text{O}_3$)**: Ultraviolet sunlight in the stratosphere splits $\text{O}_2$ into atomic oxygen, forming the **Ozone Layer**:
  $$\text{O}_2 + h\nu \to 2\text{O}, \quad \text{O} + \text{O}_2 \to \text{O}_3$$
  The ozone layer absorbs lethal solar UV-B and UV-C radiation ($200–315\text{ nm}$), which previously broke DNA bonds, finally permitting living organisms to leave the oceans and colonize dry land 450 million years ago.

### 2.2 The Carbon-Silicate Geochemical Thermostat
Earth has maintained liquid water for 4 billion years despite the Sun being 30% dimmer in the Archean (The Faint Young Sun Paradox) thanks to the **Carbonate-Silicate Cycle**:
1. Atmospheric $\text{CO}_2$ dissolves in rainwater to form weak carbonic acid ($\text{H}_2\text{CO}_3$).
2. Acid rain weathers continental silicate rocks ($\text{CaSiO}_3 + 2\text{CO}_2 + \text{H}_2\text{O} \to \text{Ca}^{2+} + 2\text{HCO}_3^- + \text{SiO}_2$).
3. Rivers carry ions to oceans, where marine organisms build shells of calcium carbonate ($\text{CaCO}_3$).
4. Shells fall to the seafloor, forming limestone rock (locking $\text{CO}_2$ in rock for millions of years).
5. Plate tectonics subducts limestone into the mantle, where extreme heat melts it, releasing $\text{CO}_2$ back through volcanic eruptions.
- **Negative Feedback**: When Earth gets too hot, weathering accelerates, drawing down $\text{CO}_2$ and cooling the planet. When Earth freezes, weathering stops while volcanoes continue releasing $\text{CO}_2$, warming the planet back up.
