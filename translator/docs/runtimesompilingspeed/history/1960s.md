# Computing and Optimization History: The 1960s & Formal Compiler Theory

## 1. Hardware Architecture & Physical Constraints
* **Representative Systems**: IBM System/360 family (1964, Gene Amdahl, Fred Brooks, Bob Evans), CDC 6600 (1964, Seymour Cray, superscalar/scoreboard architecture), PDP-8 (1965, minicomputer), Burroughs B5000 (1961, direct stack architecture).
* **Technological Shifts**:
  * **IBM System/360**: Standardized the 8-bit byte, 32-bit word, byte-addressable memory, and general-purpose register files (16 general registers `R0`–`R15`, 4 floating-point registers). Architecture was decoupled from physical implementation.
  * **CDC 6600**: The world's first supercomputer; introduced multiple independent functional units (11 parallel units: 2 adders, 2 multipliers, 1 divider, 2 shift, 1 boolean, 1 branch, 2 increment), a centralized **Scoreboard** for out-of-order instruction issue without stalling, and instruction stack buffers.
  * **Burroughs B5000**: Direct hardware execution of Algol 60 stack frames; hardware-managed call stack without general registers.

---

## 2. What Was Expensive?
* **Main Memory (Core)**: Still ranged from 32 KB to 512 KB on mainframes, and 4 KB on PDP-8 minicomputers.
* **Compilation Time and Passes**: Translating high-level programs required huge CPU time. Algol, PL/I, and Fortran IV compilers had to parse large token streams and generate code across multiple passes on tape/disk.

---

## 3. What Was Slow?
* **Instruction Issue on Single-ALU CPUs**: Serial dependencies stalled execution.
* **Complex CISC Instructions**: Multi-cycle microcoded instructions on IBM 360 (e.g., `MVC`, `ED`, `TRT`) took tens of cycles.
* **Function Call Overhead in Stack-Oriented Languages**: Saving display registers and managing activation records for recursive languages like Algol 60.

---

## 4. What Programmers Optimized Manually
* **Register Variable Packing**: Hand-allocating the 16 System/360 registers across critical loops.
* **Avoiding Stack Frames**: Algol programmers avoided recursion to prevent stack frame allocation overhead.
* **CDC 6600 Scoreboard Instruction Interleaving**: Programmers manually interleaved independent instructions (e.g., mixing floating-point multiplies with address increments) to prevent scoreboard structural and data stalls.

---

## 5. What Compilers Optimized Automatically
During the 1960s, compiler construction matured from ad-hoc pattern matching into formal mathematical theory:
* **Peephole Optimization (William M. McKeeman, 1965)**: McKeeman formalized scanning small sliding windows of generated assembly instructions (e.g., 2–4 instructions) to replace redundant load/stores (`STORE R0, X; LOAD X, R0`), simplify algebraic identities (`ADD R0, 0`), and collapse jump cascades (`JUMP L1; ... L1: JUMP L2`).
* **Control Flow Graphs (CFG) & Basic Blocks (Frances E. Allen, 1969)**: Fran Allen published *"Program Optimization"* (1969), defining the formal representation of programs as directed graphs of basic blocks, along with intervals, loops, and control dependencies.
* **Global Common Subexpression Elimination (CSE) & Dominators (John Cocke, 1969)**: Cocke introduced the dominator tree to prove when an expression calculated along one path is guaranteed to dominate and be available at another point.
* **Global Data-Flow Analysis Foundations**: Formalization of reaching definitions, available expressions, and live variable analysis over CFGs.
* **Strength Reduction via Difference Equations (Cocke & Allen, 1969)**: Mathematical formalization of converting high-degree induction polynomials into linear additions.

---

## 6. Hardware Limitations Imposed on Software
* **Lack of Dynamic Memory Allocators in Hardware**: Burroughs attempted hardware stack support, but its performance proved inferior to general-purpose register architectures when optimized by smart compilers.
* **Memory Banks & Banking Conflicts (CDC 6600)**: Cray's interleaved memory banks stalled if consecutive loads hit the same bank within 4 cycles. Compilers had to space memory accesses.

---

## 7. Breakthrough That Changed Compiler Design
* **Formal Parsing Theory (Knuth LR(k) 1965, DeRemer LALR 1969, Chomsky hierarchy)**: Compilers moved away from ad-hoc recursive parsers to automated, mathematically proven table-driven parsers.
* **Graph-Theoretic Program Optimization (Fran Allen & John Cocke, 1969–1970)**: Optimization moved from local block heuristics to global, graph-based whole-procedure analysis.

---

## 8. Breakthrough That Changed Runtime Design
* **Dijkstra’s Call Stack and Displays for Algol 60 (1960)**: Edsger W. Dijkstra, Peter Naur, and Tony Hoare established the runtime call stack, frame pointers, activation records, and display vectors for lexical scoping and nested recursion.

---

## 9. Ideas That Survived
* Control Flow Graphs (CFG) and Basic Blocks.
* Dominance and Dominance Frontiers (still the foundation of SSA today).
* Peephole optimization.
* Reaching definitions, liveness, and available expressions.
* The activation record / call stack execution model.

---

## 10. Ideas That Disappeared
* **Burroughs Pure Hardware Stack Architecture**: Direct hardware stacks made register allocation impossible for compilers, severely capping execution speed compared to register-rich ISAs.
* **Overlays**: Splitting code into manual disk-swapped memory segments before the advent of virtual memory.

---

## 11. Ideas That Returned Decades Later Under a Different Name
* **CDC 6600 Scoreboard (Parallel Functional Units)**: Reappeared in 1990s superscalar out-of-order processors (Pentium Pro, MIPS R10000) as dynamic scheduling and Reservation Stations (Tomasulo algorithm).
* **Instruction Stack Buffers (CDC 6600 8-word circular buffer)**: Returned in modern Intel/AMD processors as the **Loop Stream Detector (LSD)** and **µop cache**.
